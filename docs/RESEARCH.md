# Open research questions

The thresholds in `apple/Shared/Models.swift` (`Tuning`) are starting guesses. These are the questions that matter most:

1. **Heart-rate patterns.** How do heart rate and heart-rate variability change in the first hour after common spiking drugs (GHB, benzodiazepines, ketamine) combined with alcohol, compared with alcohol alone? What drop or rise from a person's own calm baseline is a useful trigger?
2. **Response tests.** Is a 4-target tap test sensitive to sedation and intoxication? How much slower is "impaired" for different people, and does it get worse with repetition?
3. **Movement.** What does real motion data look like for dancing, standing, sitting, slumping, and being supported or moved by another person?
4. **False alarms.** How many check-ins per night will people tolerate before switching it off?
5. **Who gets alerted.** Do friends act on texts from an unknown number at 1 a.m.? Would a venue staff contact help?

Related work:

- Smartwatch intoxication detection using accelerometer, gyroscope and heart rate (UC Irvine, 2025): arxiv.org/abs/2510.09916
- Student projects using Apple Watch data to detect intoxication and alert contacts (UCSB capstone, DUInterrupt, SafeSip).

Any study with people must go through ethics review. Never dose people for research outside a supervised clinical setting.
